Science, as both a method and an institution, occupies a paradoxical position within modern society. It promises objectivity, progress, and mastery over nature, yet it simultaneously generates profound ethical dilemmas. The relentless pursuit of innovation often outpaces moral reflection, leaving humanity to confront consequences it is insufficiently prepared to manage. From genetic modification to artificial intelligence, scientific advancement continually expands the boundaries of what can be achieved, while offering limited guidance on what ought to be pursued. This widening gap between capability and conscience underscores the urgent need for ethical frameworks that evolve alongside discovery. Historically, science has been portrayed as a neutral enterprise, its moral value determined solely by human application. However, this claim of neutrality has become increasingly untenable in a world where data systems, algorithms, and biotechnologies actively shape social realities. The scientist no longer functions merely as an observer of phenomena but as a transformative agent capable of altering ecosystems, economies, and even the structure of human life. Consequently, responsibility becomes inseparable from inquiry itself, and the act of experimentation carries implications that extend beyond laboratories. Ethics, therefore, cannot remain external to scientific practice; it must be embedded within its methodology, governance, and dissemination. The development of nuclear weapons, pervasive surveillance systems, and environmentally destructive technologies illustrates how knowledge divorced from accountability can result in existential risk. Scientific progress must be accompanied by transparency, interdisciplinary dialogue, and public engagement. Only when innovation is guided by moral deliberation can science truly serve humanity rather than endanger it.
